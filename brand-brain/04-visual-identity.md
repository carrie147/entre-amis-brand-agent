### Accessibility check `PROPOSED`

Contrast ratios checked against WCAG AA (4.5:1 for normal text):

- **Text colours on cream or white: all pass** (4.8–8.5:1).
- **Text colour on its own fill: all fail** (1.5–2.5:1). Never set a family's
  text colour on its fill.
- **White text on fills:** only Wild passes for normal text (5.4:1). Word Play
  (4.2), Mischief (3.9), Secret Mission (3.6), Savour (3.5), Wine Knowledge
  (3.1) are OK only for large or bold text (3:1). Pour (2.6) fails — never
  white text on Pour.

## Typography

### Typeface `CONFIRMED`

**Raleway** — in **Light** (300) and **SemiBold / Bold** (600 / 700).

No other typefaces. The logo is artwork, not typed out — never recreate it in
Raleway or any other font.

`CONFIRMED` **In the app, small reading text uses Raleway Regular (400)**, because Light is
too thin to read at phone size. Light stays for large text and display lines (founder,
24 October 2026).

### Print type scale `PROPOSED`

From the Master v1.0 consistency review (5 November 2026). The founder asked
for the type scale to be made right, and the creative masters now follow it;
the scale itself is for confirmation.

- **Sizes (pt):** 7 · 8 · 9 · 11 · 14 · 18 · 24 · 36, with 48, 60 and 72 for
  display. Nothing below 7 pt.
- **Small text is deep olive.** Mid olive text on cream (2.95:1) and blush
  text on cream (1.65:1) fail for normal text. Mid olive only at 24 pt and
  above; blush text only on deep olive, large or bold.
- **Light italic only from 10 pt.** Below that, use Regular italic.
- **Signage and slides** keep their own sizes (they are read from a
  distance); the colour rules still apply.
- Open point for the founder: `07` (also `PROPOSED`) asks for 9 pt minimum on
  captions and 10.5 pt for instructions read during play. The current
  masters set the How to Play body at 9 pt and the QR card's small lines at
  7 pt. One of the two needs to give.

### How to use the weights `PROPOSED`

| Use | Weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large headings, display lines, brand line | Light | Airy and premium — as on the deck |
| Short labels, small-caps headings (ANY WINE) | SemiBold | Letter-spaced, as on the deck and How to Play |
| Emphasis, buttons, card family names, cork values | SemiBold / Bold | |
| Body copy | Light, at a comfortable size | Light gets hard to read when small or on dark colours — see below |

**Watch-outs**

- **Light at small sizes.** Light is thin; at small sizes, on screens, or on
  dark or coloured fills (e.g. card families) it can become hard to read.
  Keep Light for larger text and use SemiBold for small text there.
- **Numbers.** Raleway's default numbers are "old-style" (they rise and dip
  like lowercase letters). For scores, cork counts, prices and anything in a
  column, switch on lining figures (`font-variant-numeric: lining-nums`) so
  numbers sit level.

## App interface foundations

One set of interface rules for every part of the app (game, Partner, Event,
Just tasting, My wines, Wine 101), locked one at a time: top bar and
navigation first, then home, how the experience works, and how the brand is
applied. Background: the founder's requirement of 27 November 2026 for one
consistent design language, and the *Integrated App UI, Stage 1 Visual
Review* (DRAFT, 27 November 2026).

`CONFIRMED` **How these rules work** (founder, 27 November 2026):

- Any screen found that does not follow a confirmed rule is an exception and
  is amended to match the rule.
- Any problem with applying a rule is raised with the founder and discussed.
  Claude never decides it.
- No rolling back. The built app and earlier decisions stay as they are until
  the development brief applies these rules.

### The style (sheet 12.7)

**The feeling: one world, two distances.** Friends around wine and food,
caught in the middle of a conversation. The viewer has just sat down at the
table. Conversation matters more than faces, and more than the wine.

- **Close** (Boards 1 and 3): three to eight people, or one glass, in tight
  crops that also work in the app.
- **Stepped back** (Board 2): longer tables, a visible host, the place becomes
  part of the story. The same light and the same people in both.

**Light and colour**

- Low sun, open shade or candlelight. Soft, warm, natural.
- Natural colour that sits with olive, cream and blush. No heavy orange casts,
  nothing over-vivid.
- Real food, linen, wood and greenery. Settings that feel European without
  being postcard views.
- Space in the frame for a crop or a line of text.
- Sharp and full resolution: no softness, banding or upscaled phone shots.

**Camera and moment**

- Eye level, often over a guest's shoulder, so the viewer is at the table.
- People look at each other, never at the lens.
- Moments in the middle of a story: listening, reacting, laughing, passing a
  dish.
- Hands and gestures carry a lot of the picture.
- Glasses sit at the edge of the frame, not in the middle of it.

**Casting**

- Mid 30s to 40s for any new picture; a real mix of backgrounds.
- Friends, not models: natural, not styled to perfection.
- Nobody who is, or might look, under 25.
- No AI-generated people, ever.

**The wine**

- One glass in view per person. Final picks keep a single glass in shot where
  the wine is the subject.
- Bottles never the hero. No readable third-party labels.
- Food on the table; no overpouring, nothing that suggests drinking too much.

**Light and shadow (Board 3 detail).** Within Board 3, one or two still lifes
where the light does the work: a glass and its shadow on linen or a warm,
plain wall. No face, lots of space, one idea. A quiet accent, not a strand of
its own. References: 23 (Board 3) and R1.

- **R1 is a style reference only.** It is a social media screenshot (The House
  Outfit), not licensed, and must never be used. Its production standard is
  too low for use in any case.

**What we are not**

- Toasts aimed at the camera; bottles as the hero.
- Tastings that feel like a class or an exam (31); barrel rooms.
- Conferences, networking, crowded parties.
- "Old friends" who are literally old (02).
- One person carrying the picture (01); unclear focus (08).
- Busy backgrounds (29); over-vivid colour (20).
- Festive props; lavender and straw-hat clichés.
- Anyone who might look under 25.

Every picture turned down is on sheet 12.6.
