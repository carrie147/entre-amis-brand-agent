Source: “ENTRE AMIS – The Full Story – on Master v1.0 – DRAFT 6 Nov 2026” (Google Slides, revision 8pyXnGZu5jyI4Q). The deck was read only. Nothing in it has been changed.

Five points first

1. It is two decks in one

Slides 1–20, 37–40 and 49–51 tell an outward-facing story. Slides 21–36 and 41–48 are marked INTERNAL and include equity, economics and hypotheses. None of the internal slides should be in front of Stephen. Pete, as a shareholder, can see them in a separate appendix.

2. The product story is strong; the evidence is thin

The game, the app and Partner Mode are explained well. Everything about value to producers is illustrative (slides 26, 27) or not yet claimable (23, 51). This is honest, but it means the meeting has to be framed as exploration, not proof.

3. Nothing shows where we actually are

No slide says “built / being tested privately / not yet validated” in one place. The controlled beta has been live since 6 November, and no invited tester has played a game yet (usage counts, 9 November). A Progress slide is the main gap.

4. Nothing asks Stephen for anything

The deck has no slide about what we would like from him. Slide 38 (“looking for founding partners”) is the nearest. One closing slide with two or three options is needed, once you and Pete have agreed the objective.

5. Four facts to check before anything is shared

Slide 47: equity and trade mark date. Slide 48: “company formed” and “work live”. Slide 41: SPIEL Essen figures unsourced. Slide 3: a respondent’s words are quoted. Details in the concerns list below.

The deck has 51 slides, not 50: the 6 November pass inserted “Early consumer insight” as slide 3. The numbers below are the slide positions, which match the page numbers printed on the slides. Your message was cut off partway through Task 3 (“Why it might be worth…”). I have read it as asking why the conversation might be worth Stephen’s time and involvement.

Task 3: the commercial story from Stephen’s side

Why it matters to producers

Clear. Slides 15 and 16 explain the social layer: conversation turns a tasting into taking part, the wines are remembered in My wines, and the producer gets anonymised insight with consent. This is the strongest part of the deck for a wine-industry reader.

How it creates value

Partly clear. Engagement and insight are described. A senior producer will ask, “does it sell more wine, grow my direct customers, or my club?”. The deck rightly avoids claiming that, but it should say what a pilot would measure (for example: saves, crowns, opted-in contacts, return visits). That is an honest answer to the question.

Fit with a producer brand

Gap. Partner Mode is written for cellar doors, venues and clubs; Event Mode for festivals. A brand like Mirabeau may care as much about consumer occasions and brand storytelling at home as about a tasting room. Ask Stephen where his customers meet the brand, rather than assume.

Why it is worth his time

Not yet stated. The reasons are early access to shape it, a low-commitment way to try something new with consumers, and the founder’s experience (49). These need to be said on the final slide, in plain words, without implying traction we do not have.

Material risks if shown as it stands

  1. Invented numbers read as results (26, 27, 34). These are the biggest credibility risk with an industry expert.
  2. Registration data read as validation (3). Nine invited contacts, and no tester has completed a game yet.
  3. Prototype read as product (12, 48 “work live”). Partner and Event Mode have not been used by a partner.
  4. Internal content leaking (cover label, TO DECIDE/TO CONFIRM boxes on 14, 23, 24, 28, 29, 51; equity and economics).
  5. Unverified facts (41 market figures; 47 trade mark date and equity before a company exists).

On the four possible objectives (your decision)

An observation, not a recommendation to adopt. The evidence today supports routes 1 and 2 in a first conversation: introductions and advice, and an exploratory pilot discussion. Routes 3 (investment) and 4 (licensing or acquisition) would normally need validated demand, a formed company and agreed economics, and none of these exist yet. Raising them now could weaken the first meeting. A common approach is to lead with advice and introductions, explore a pilot, and listen for whether Stephen raises investment himself. You and Pete should decide this at the preparation meeting.
